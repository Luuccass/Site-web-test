# Restaurant website benchmark (2026-10-06)

Web-search synthesis only: no site could be opened (WebFetch blocked). See caveats at the end.

TOP:
 - Onyx Restaurant Paris (Awwwards Nominee, Aug 2025) | https://www.awwwards.com/sites/onyx-restaurant-paris | The strongest evidence that a small French bistronomie site can be recognised. I re-ran the search and the Awwwards page exists ('a bistronomic restaurant nestled in the heart of Paris', from snippet). The restaurant takes its name and identity from one physical feature, a 4 m onyx wall (sortiraparis and luxe-infinity, from snippet). It also sets out two clearly priced offers, a 35 € lunch and a separate evening menu, with bookings through OpenTable. For Comme Avant: build the identity on one real material or place (the slate, the magnolias, the lane by the church) and present price-anchored entry points. The Boite à Oeufs credit appeared in one raw snippet but not in my check, so cite it with care. The site's layout, type and motion were NOT seen.
 - Bernice Bakery by Casa Media (Awwwards Site of the Month) | https://www.awwwards.com/bonjour-casa/ | I confirmed it in my own search. A warm neighbourhood shop (Saint-Henri, Montréal) won at the highest Awwwards level. Casa Media's case study (https://casamedia.com/?p=18854, from snippet) describes the aim as recreating the feeling of visiting the shop: 'warmth and texture', 'soft tones, generous white space, product-focused photography'. It also claims a 25% rise in sales, a figure the agency reports itself. This is the best proof that a site can win an award by being warm and focused on the product rather than technically flashy. The year is unclear (probably around 2022) and the site is outside the 2023–26 window.
 - Gelato La Boca by Matchbox Studio (2025 Webby Winner, Food & Drink) | https://winners.webbyawards.com/2025/websites-and-mobile-sites/general-desktop-mobile-sites/food-drink/322326/gelato-la-boca-website | A very small independent food shop won the category. The credited team includes a senior copywriter and an illustrator, Kyle Steed (from snippet). That suggests voice and hand-made assets count as much as engineering, which supports giving Comme Avant the couple's own voice in the first person and one hand-made layer. Design details were NOT seen.
 - Awwwards restaurant 'inspiration elements': Flavori contacts, Völlerei menu, Socca reserve-a-table, 1nul8 and Zur Linde footers | https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/contacts-flavori-restaurant | The curators single out the practical parts of restaurant sites as craft: contacts (Flavori, Honorable Mention, 4 Jul 2024, by Max Vasilyev), menu and scroll (Völlerei by dreist, https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/voellerei-menu), reserve-a-table (https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/reserve-a-table-socca-bistro) and footers (https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/footer-1nul8-restaurant-wine-bar, https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/restaurant-footer-zur-linde-rotkreuz). The lesson: the parts that convert visitors can also be the parts that win awards. Only the titles came from snippets; what these components look like is unknown.
 - Galvin Restaurants by PGWD (Awwwards inspiration: 'seamless booking', What's On) | https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/seemless-booking-galvin-restaurants-1 | From snippet: the booking flow is a designed screen rather than a pasted-in widget, and a 'What's On' calendar gives people a reason to come back. For Comme Avant this becomes a 'Ce moment-ci' module (broche du jour, gibier, soirée Rhône) with a booking flow inside the brand. Live site not seen.
 - BAVN, Aarhus (Vokseværk, Awwwards Nominee, Feb 2025) | https://www.awwwards.com/sites/bavn | A traditional, seasonal house similar to Comme Avant. Its brief, quoted in the snippet, is 'simplicity and delicious details in focus to focus on content and the experience'. One site serves two offers in the same building, which suggests a salle/terrasse or midi/soir switch instead of separate pages. Design not seen.
 - Le Bouchon Alsacien, Lyon: reverse-glass gilded sign (Fonts In Use) | https://fontsinuse.com/uses/72212/le-bouchon-alsacien-lyon | The most local precedent for craft (Lyon). Engraving and gold leaf on deep blue glass, from snippet. This French sign-making tradition gives a credible 'gold on dark' for ONE hero moment (the wordmark) and fits the slate photography. Repeated across the site it turns into luxury kitsch.
 - Le Cavin, 'cave à manger et à boire', Bagnères-de-Bigorre (Fonts In Use) | https://www.fontsinuse.com/uses/36679/cave-a-manger-et-a-boire | The closest analogue to Comme Avant: a provincial town, a cellar at the heart of the house. Its type works in three layers (from snippet): a sturdy, slightly imperfect display face (Jean-Luc), a readable traditional serif for the carte (Marion), and hand lettering kept for content that changes. That layering can be lifted directly into a web type system.
 - La Tour d'Argent identity by ZeCraft (Fonts In Use) | https://www.fontsinuse.com/uses/72113/tour-d-argent | The logotype is redrawn from the restaurant's own archive (the Vendôme typeface it had used, 1950s style). It pairs Le Monde Livre Classic (Typofonderie) for menus with Darby Sans (from snippet). For a name that means 'as before', the method transfers: redraw the lettering from real Dardilly-le-Bas sources (church, street plaques, old shopfronts) and pair a French text serif for the carte with one quiet sans for the practical UI. Ignore the luxury register.
 - Hors Cadre bistro by Studio Matte (World Brand Design) | https://worldbranddesign.com/?p=462733 | A flexible logotype: the gap between the two words widens to hold slogans, and loose hand-drawn sketches give the place an intimate feel (from snippet). Comme Avant also has two words, so the space between 'Comme' and 'Avant' could hold the line of the day, giving a living headline that stays inside the identity. Its burgundy and cream palette is the default bistro palette, so treat it as a warning.
 - Frenchette by Mucca (Fonts In Use) | https://www.fontsinuse.com/uses/36390/frenchette | The guiding principle: a French restaurant that does not 'try too hard to be French' (from snippet). The Frenchness should come from the language, correct French typesetting and craft, not from national symbols. Typefaces were not named in the snippet.
 - Paris bistro ardoise hand-lettering (Print, Daily Heller) | https://www.printmag.com/daily-heller/we-dont-have-to-use-no-stinkin-designer/ | Paris bistro chalkboards are lettered by the owner-chef, for example at L'Ange Gardien (from snippet). This supports a REAL daily ardoise from Fabrice or Magali, photographed or scanned each day with live text alongside, instead of a fake chalk font. It is the most honest and appetising 'fresh today' signal available.

DEVICES:
 - One real material or place as the organising idea (Onyx's onyx wall). For Comme Avant, choose ONE: the black slate (from the photography), the magnolia terrace, or the lane down from the church. Let it set the palette, the texture of the section dividers and the microcopy.
 - A redrawn wordmark from local sources (Tour d'Argent's archive method). Photograph old lettering in Dardilly-le-Bas and redraw 'Comme Avant' from it rather than buying a script font.
 - A single gilded-glass moment (Le Bouchon Alsacien, Lyon). The hero wordmark is shown as gold leaf on near-black, with one slow specular sweep that runs once and a static frame under prefers-reduced-motion. The rest of the site stays matte, so the gold reads as a village sign and not a nightclub.
 - A gap in the logotype that holds the line of the day (Hors Cadre): 'Comme · broche de volaille ce midi · Avant'. The headline stays alive and is fed from the same data as the ardoise.
 - Three type layers (Le Cavin, Le Boucanier). A French text serif for dishes and story. A condensed grotesque in small caps for course headings and for the appellations in the cave (CÔTE-RÔTIE · MORGON · GIVRY), dense and easy to scan. Hand lettering ONLY on the real daily ardoise. Prefer French foundries (Production Type, Typofonderie, Bodhuin, Margot Lévêque) as a quiet authenticity signal.
 - A real ardoise as live content. The owners' chalkboard is scanned or photographed each day and shown with a dated caption ('Ardoise du mardi 6 octobre') and live text for screen readers and search.
 - Occasion-based entry points (Chez Ly, Onyx's lunch/evening split): Déjeuner (a price-anchored lunch), Dîner, Dimanche en famille, Terrasse sous les magnolias. Each one goes straight to booking.
 - A two-mood switch (BAVN's two offers in one building): Midi/Soir or Salle/Terrasse. It defaults from the current Europe/Paris time and changes the photography and the tone of the copy, never the information architecture.
 - Designed practical components as award material (the Awwwards inspiration elements): an editorial HTML carte, a booking flow inside the brand, a directions block styled as an enamel street plaque ('Dardilly-le-Bas · à côté de l'église'), and a footer that closes like a scene with the phone number set large.
 - 'Ce moment-ci' module (Galvin's What's On): gibier en saison, soirée vins du Rhône, Chartreuse desserts in winter. It gives people a reason to return and keeps search content fresh.
 - Hand-made layer plus a strong copy voice (Gelato La Boca's credits). Notes signed by Magali and Fabrice in the first person, and a few ink sketches of the house, the magnolia and the bell tower used as favicon, dividers and an illustrated map of the lane.
 - A chromatic signature drawn from the menu: a Chartreuse-green accent used ONLY for the dessert chapter and the one primary CTA. Wine red appears only in the cave. Slate black, warm cream and a muted gold carry the brand. (This is a design recommendation, not taken from a reference.)
 - French typesetting as identity (Frenchette, Chez Olivier): « guillemets », a non-breaking space before ; : ! ?, small caps for appellations, prices aligned on tabular figures.

UX:
 - A sticky 3-slot thumb bar on mobile: Réserver (primary) / Appeler (tel:, labelled with who answers and when) / Venir (deep link to Apple or Google Maps for the pedestrian lane, with a parking hint). It respects safe-area-inset-bottom, is hidden on /reserver, and appears after the first scroll so the hero keeps its first impression. On desktop a quiet 'Réserver' sits top right. Sticky action bars are a standard feature of restaurant site builders (Sociavore docs, from snippet); I found no data on how much they lift conversion.
 - Calling is a primary channel in France. The HOTREC/GHR study found 88.3% of bookings are made directly and 64.6% by phone (https://www.ghr.fr/europe-numerique/plateformes-en-ligne/restaurants/etude-sur-la-distribution-en-ligne-des-restaurants-en-france-et-en-europe, from snippet, probably pre-2020). Show the number as text, not only as an icon.
 - A live status line computed in Europe/Paris time: 'Ouvert · service du midi jusqu'à 14h' / 'Fermé · réouverture jeudi 12h' / 'En congés jusqu'au …'. It reads one hours file that includes services, weekly closing days, French public holidays and congés. The same file feeds openingHoursSpecification and specialOpeningHoursSpecification in the JSON-LD and is copied by hand to the Google Business Profile.
 - The carte as HTML, never a PDF. 'La carte' is the first navigation item and has a teaser above the fold (TouchBistro 2024: 85% of US diners check the menu online, from snippet). On mobile, a sticky horizontal chip nav with scroll-spy (Entrées · Broche du jour · Plats · Fromages · Desserts · Cave). On desktop, a sticky serif index. Text at 16 px or more, prices aligned, a dated 'Ardoise / Broche du jour' block at the top, and a print stylesheet in place of the PDF. It must be readable without JavaScript.
 - The 14 EU allergens (décret 2015-447 / INCO, Keobiz sheet, from snippet): an 'Afficher les allergènes' toggle that shows a code on each dish with a sticky legend, plus the line 'Tableau complet disponible au restaurant'. Whether the decree strictly applies to a showcase site was not confirmed, so treat this as best practice.
 - Honest booking requests if confirmation is manual. The button says 'Envoyer ma demande'. Fields: date, midi/soir, number of covers, name, phone, optional note (terrasse, allergies, occasion). The success screen says when and how the Guillons will confirm. Same-day requests and groups of 8 or more are steered to a call. No captcha wall, no account, email optional. The Google Business Profile booking link points to the site's /reserver.
 - The cave as a curated teaser: three to five picks each for Rhône, Beaujolais and Bourgogne, plus 'Plus de 100 références, conseil de Fabrice, sur place'. Never a table of 100 rows on mobile.
 - A quiet proof block next to the CTA: the live Google rating and review count, linked to Google, plus two or three short dated quotes. No auto-rotating carousel.
 - Restaurant JSON-LD: name, address, geo of the lane, telephone, servesCuisine, priceRange €€, acceptsReservations → /reserver, hasMenu → /carte marked up with Menu/MenuSection/MenuItem in EUR. Name, address and phone identical on the site and the Google Business Profile (Geolid 2026: 69% of profile visits come from Search, from snippet).
 - A performance budget that suits low-key photography: LCP under 2.5 s on 4G, AVIF/WebP with srcset, only the hero preloaded, no hero video, no preloader, any motion or WebGL lazy-loaded after first interaction, and a static map image that links to Maps instead of an iframe. Google/SOASTA: going from 1 s to 3 s raises bounce probability by 32%.
 - Photography that makes people hungry: keep the specular highlights and the colour on the food, avoid crushed blacks, show real dishes (broche, foie gras maison, Chartreuse desserts) and the real couple, and include at least one daylight terrace or magnolia image to balance the dark palette (MGH: 45% of diners look for food photos and 36% have been put off by them; 2019 US survey).
 - Accessibility: AA contrast or better on dark surfaces (cream on slate, never grey on slate), tap targets of 44 px or more, prefers-reduced-motion respected, native scroll everywhere, no hover-only content, and no newsletter or cookie modal covering the CTAs on the first visit.
 - Content the owners can edit: the ardoise, the broche du jour, the 'ce moment-ci' module, congés and the carte all come from a simple CMS or JSON that Magali or Fabrice can update in under two minutes from a phone. If the content is not maintained, the 'live' concept turns into a liability.

CLICHES:
 - A generic restaurant template: a full-screen stock food slider, 'Bienvenue sur notre site', and three 'À propos / Menu / Contact' cards.
 - A full-screen autoplay video hero or a preloader with a percentage counter that delays the phone number, hours and carte.
 - A PDF menu or a photo of the menu as the only carte.
 - Booking or the phone number reachable only from a hamburger menu or a Contact page.
 - Scroll-jacking, horizontal-scroll galleries, heavy smooth-scroll on mobile, custom cursors, hover-only reveals.
 - Common Awwwards-template effects: headlines revealed letter by letter, film grain and noise on every surface, a magnetic button on every CTA, WebGL distortion on food photos (it makes the food unappetising).
 - AI-looking defaults: a purple-to-teal gradient, glassmorphism cards, Poppins or Inter as the whole voice, rounded-2xl cards with soft drop shadows, emoji icons (including emoji allergen icons), bento grids, generic AI-generated food or interior images.
 - Postcard Frenchness: Eiffel Tower, berets, Breton stripes, tricolour accents, red-and-white gingham, accordion music, 'Bon appétit!' in giant script, 'Ratatouille' whimsical script logo.
 - Lyon bouchon kitsch: cartoon pigs or chefs, 'Lyon capitale de la gastronomie' slogans.
 - Fake chalk fonts and chalk-texture backgrounds. If there is an ardoise, it should be a real one written by the owners.
 - Gold on black everywhere, hairline serifs and 'expérience gastronomique' copy. These are fine-dining codes that misprice a 20–50 € cuisine-maison house and turn the slate photography into a nightclub.
 - Borrowing the Paris néo-brasserie look (Belle Époque gilt, mosaics, red velvet) or bright pop 3D lettering (Boucherie, Boucan registers).
 - Burgundy and cream as the whole brand, since it is the default bistro palette. Keep wine red for the cave.
 - Pastiche nostalgia: distressed vintage badges, 'Depuis 19XX' seals, kraft paper, coffee stains, wine-ring illustrations. 'Comme Avant' should feel remembered, not costumed.
 - The 1970s 'wonky' soft display serif that has been overused by restaurant brands since about 2020. (This is my opinion; I could not source it.)
 - Agency-speak: 'immersive journey', 'culinary experience', 'art de vivre'. Use the couple's plain voice instead.
 - 'Réservation confirmée' copy on what is actually a manual request; auto-rotating five-star carousels; embedded Instagram grids; a heavy Google Maps iframe as the only way to get directions.
 - Crushed-black low-key photos in which the dish disappears into the slate.

SEEDS:
 - L'ARDOISE (slate as the system). Dark slate surfaces from the reference photography, with a warm cream type layer and the food lit like the photos. The centre of the homepage is the REAL daily ardoise, written by Fabrice or Magali, scanned and dated, with live text beside it. The ardoise feeds the line held in the logotype gap ('Comme · broche de pintade ce midi · Avant'), the open/closed status and the 'Broche du jour' block at the top of the HTML carte. Type: a French text serif for dishes, a condensed grotesque in small caps for courses and appellations. The one flourish: the wordmark as gilded glass on black, swept once. Why it can win: the site visibly changes every day because the restaurant does, and the concept, the content and the conversion path are the same object. Risk: it depends on the owners keeping it up, so it needs a phone-friendly two-minute update flow and a graceful fallback ('Ardoise du jour à découvrir sur place').
 - LA RUELLE (arrival as narrative). The page follows the actual walk in: the church square, the pedestrian lane, the door of the renovated house, the salle and the broche, the magnolia terrace, the cellar. It ends at the footer, which is the directions. The structure is a native vertical scroll with chapter markers. A hand-inked map of the lane, drawn as one continuous line, threads through the sections and becomes the static map in the footer that links to Maps. Address, parking and '10 min de Lyon' sit on a single enamel street-plaque component in a village palette rather than Paris blue and green. Why it can win: the directions, the strongest conversion moment for a village 10 minutes from Lyon, become the hero of the concept, much as Awwwards curates contacts and footers. Risk: it must not hide the carte or the booking behind the story, so the thumb bar is always present and the carte teaser sits above the fold.
 - SOUS LES MAGNOLIAS (midi / soir). A two-mood site switched by time of day in Europe/Paris time, with a manual toggle. MIDI: cream paper, daylight terrace photos, magnolia blossom, the price-anchored lunch first. SOIR: slate black, low-key candle-lit plates, the cave and the Chartreuse desserts first, with a Chartreuse-green accent kept for the desserts and the primary CTA. Same information architecture and content, different light. One ink magnolia emblem serves as favicon, divider and booking-confirmation mark. Why it can win: a clear concept that grows out of the real place (terrace plus dark-photography reference) and resolves the tension between dark and appetising. Risk: the two modes must not duplicate maintenance, so only the photography, the order of sections and the tone change.
 - LE CARNET DE LA MAISON (archive-led, 'as before'). The identity is redrawn from lettering found in Dardilly-le-Bas (church inscriptions, street plaques, any old sign on the house), following Tour d'Argent's archive method. The site reads like the couple's notebook: first-person notes signed by Magali and Fabrice, small dated entries in a 'ce moment-ci' module (gibier, soirée Rhône, première broche de l'automne), a carte set with French typographic care (« », thin spaces, small-caps appellations), and a cave grouped as Rhône / Beaujolais / Bourgogne with Fabrice's picks. Hand-made layer: pencil or ink sketches, never stock. Why it can win: like the Gelato La Boca Webby, voice and hand-made assets lead and engineering stays invisible. Risk: it can slide into sepia nostalgia, so keep the photography contemporary and dark-warm, with crisp layout and no distressed textures.
 - LA BROCHE (one kinetic signature, use sparingly). The rotisserie is the only motion idea: a single glyph or line drawing of the spit that turns slowly, once, beside the 'Broche du jour' plaque, which is a slightly embossed enamel or metal label in the spirit of shop signs. Everything else is static and editorial. Why it might work: it is ownable and appetising, it is literally what the house does, and it marks freshness. Risk: it is the most likely of the five to become a gimmick, so treat it as an accent to combine with seeds 1 or 4, never as the main concept, and freeze it under reduced motion.
